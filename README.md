# Decentralized Document Verification
## Proving that a document is what you say it is and from who you say it is from. 
Say you are a recent graduate, applying to jobs. You might want to share the grades you received in certain classes without sharing your entire transcript with perspective employers. You could tell the perspective employers your grade but without having a copy of the official transcripts there's no proof. What if, instead of just sharing your transcripts, you broke up the transcript into a merkle tree, allowing you to prove individual grades without showing the whole thing. This still isn't enough though. The employer still doesn't know for sure that you didn't just make it up or edit the document before sending the merkle proof. The solution to this is thus: The university itself makes the merkle root of the tree publicly visible, along with a signed version which can be verified with a publicly shared public key. This way the employer knows for sure that the document comes from the school, and the scores you tell them are accurate. All you would need to do is generate a merkle proof for the data you want to reveal (likely the classes you want to show and their scores, along with your name so they know it isn't someone elses). 

This is all well and good, but it can be improved for more use cases. Say you wanted to commit a signed contract so that you and the other party both can be sure that the other one won't try to claim that the agreement was different at a later date. This may or may not need to be made usable for selective disclosure, but that is not the only issue: What if one party to the contract really wants to change it and is able to bribe the owner of the service storing the hashes to allow them to make changes. The owner of the service shouldn't be trusted absolutely either, as it still allows a weakness. This is why the document's hash is not only uploaded to this third party service, but it is also anchored in some way to a public blockchain. When you upload the contract, you are given a "receipt" which lets you know where the on-chain anchor is, so that even if the service owner is willing to manipulate data, the decentralized system makes that impossible to do without getting caught.

This is what this project plans to do: create a system whereby documents can be uploaded for later verification in a way which 
1. doesn't actual expose the contents of the documents publicly, 
2. allows selective disclosure, 
3. ensures that the data cannot be forged/edited at a later date.
